## 01 · Overview

**Section label:** `Project Overview`

**H2:** `The brief was 0 to 1: define the product, then make people believe in it.`

**Body:**
> Paira started as a question that every young professional feels but few tools answer well:
> how do you reach the right person when cold outreach fails and every platform is noisy? I
> joined early enough to help answer it at the level of product direction, not just screens.
> I helped define what Paira was for, who it served, and the core value proposition it would
> defend: **stop searching profiles, start stating intent.**

**H2 (second lede):** `The insight the whole product rests on.`

**Body:**
> People do not want more connections. They want the right one, at the moment they have a real
> need. So Paira is built around a request, not a profile. You say what you want in a sentence
> ("a referral at a product company," "a mock PM interview," "a resume review from a UX
> designer"), and the product's job is to make the right introduction happen with as little
> friction as possible.

**IMAGE — problem framing:** a simple 2x2 or row of the four failure modes of networking today
(hard to find the right people · cold outreach fails · fragmented platforms · noisy signals).
Can be a clean graphic in your system, not a screenshot.

## 02 · The Pivot  *(your signature section — lead with this in interviews)*

**Section label:** `The Pivot`

**H2:** `We were building a better profile app. I helped turn it into an agent.`

**Body:**
> The early Paira looked like a familiar answer: swipeable profiles, matching, a networking
> feed. It tested fine, but it inherited the same problem it was trying to solve. Matching on
> profiles still left people staring at a blank first message. So I drove a strategic pivot,
> from a profile-matching app to an **agentic, request-driven marketplace for help.** The unit
> of the product stopped being a person and became a request.

**Body (what changed, concretely):**
> The shift rewrote the core flows I owned. Instead of browsing people, you post a need in one
> sentence. AI agents refine it, surface the right person, and even open the conversation, so
> the hardest part of networking, the first message, is handled for you. Instead of being stuck
> as a mentor or a mentee, you switch roles by request. Instead of a cold intro, every match
> arrives with the context of why.

**IMAGE — before / after:** the clearest possible side by side. Left: the pre-pivot
profile/swipe concept. Right: the shipped request + AI-agent flow. This single comparison is
the most valuable image on the page.

**Pull quote (from the live product, true and on-message):**
> "Skip the networking. Get a company referral." — Paira's own promise, post-pivot.

## 03 · Core AI Flows  *(what I designed)*

**Section label:** `Core Experience`

**H2:** `Three flows carry the product. I designed each from concept to shipped screen.`

**Flow 01 — AI Matching**
> Start with one sentence. AI agents refine your request, recommend the right people, and speed
> up the repetitive parts of a conversation. The design problem was trust: an agent acting on
> your behalf has to feel legible, so every recommendation shows its reason.

**Flow 02 — Request Marketplace**
> The heart of the pivot. People post specific, time-bound needs with a category, a focus area,
> and an optional reward. The lifecycle I designed runs Post → Browse → Respond → Select → Chat,
> so a vague "let's connect" becomes a concrete exchange with a clear finish line.

**Flow 03 — Rewards & role-switching**
> Help is a two-way market. I designed the flow that lets someone mentor on one request and ask
> for help on the next, and earn meaningful rewards for the time they give. It is what keeps
> supply and demand alive on the same platform.

**IMAGE — per flow:** one clean phone frame each (AI Matching screen, a Request card + its
lifecycle, the Rewards / role-switch screen). Three portrait 9:19.5 frames, or one wide triptych.

**IMAGE — flow diagram (optional, strong):** the Request lifecycle as a small 5-step diagram in
your system (Post · Browse · Respond · Select · Chat).

## 06 · Growth & Validation

**Section label:** `Growth & Validation`

**H2:** `I designed the flows that get people in and bring them back, then tuned them on real feedback.`

**Body:**
> Product direction is only half of 0→1. I designed and iterated the growth and retention flows:
> onboarding, referral, and the premium features. Then I tuned them against continuous feedback
> from **100+ waitlist users and 50+ beta testers**, watching where people stalled and cutting
> the friction out of the first run.

**Key iterations (scannable list):**
- Simplified the onboarding so a new user reaches their first request fast.
- Refined the request structure so needs are specific enough to match.
- Added AI-assisted tag generation so people describe intent without effort.
- Shaped referral and premium so growth and value reinforce each other.

**IMAGE — onboarding strip:** 3–4 onboarding frames in sequence.
